\section{Adaptive Action Chunking}
\label{sec:adaptive_chunking}

\subsection{Motivation}

The standard Flow Matching objective applies a uniform mean-squared-error (MSE) loss to
every step of the action chunk, implicitly assuming that all timesteps within the chunk
are equally important. In robotic manipulation, however, the critical motion moments ---
such as making contact or reversing motion direction --- are concentrated in only a few
steps of the chunk. Weighting these steps identically to the quasi-static steps leaves
the model under-trained precisely where fine control matters most. We therefore propose
\textbf{Adaptive Action Chunking}, which reweights the flow-matching loss according to
the per-step change rate of the ground-truth actions, and introduces an auxiliary
boundary prediction head that makes the model explicitly aware of the transition
structure inside an action chunk. The module is optional: when disabled, it recovers the
standard uniform-MSE objective and remains fully compatible with the original training
setup.

\subsection{Preliminaries}

Let $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_a \times d_a}$ denote the normalized ground-truth action
chunk, where $T_a$ is the number of action steps and $d_a$ the per-step action dimension.
Under the conditional flow-matching framework, the model predicts a velocity field
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}_\tau$ with regression target
$\mathbf{u}_{t,\tau} = \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\tau - \mathbf{a}_\tau$, and the baseline loss
is a uniformly weighted MSE. Our contribution lies in the per-step reweighting of this
loss and the auxiliary boundary supervision.

\subsection{Change-Rate Weighting}

We measure how abruptly the action evolves by the L2 distance between consecutive action
steps. The per-step change rate is defined as
%
\begin{equation}
  c_\tau =
  \begin{cases}
    \|\mathbf{a}_2 - \mathbf{a}_1\|_2, & \tau = 1,\\[2pt]
    \|\mathbf{a}_\tau - \mathbf{a}_{\tau-1}\|_2, & \tau = 2, \ldots, T_a,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:change_rate}
\end{equation}
%
i.e., we take the L2 norm of the $T_a - 1$ consecutive (backward) differences over a chunk
of length $T_a$ and prepend a copy of the first difference so that the change rate aligns
with the action sequence length (for a chunk of length $T_a = 1$ the change rate
degenerates to all zeros). The change rate is then normalized per sample by its own
maximum:
%
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{c}_\tau
  = \frac{c_\tau}{\max\!\bigl(\max_{\tau'} c_{\tau'},\ \varepsilon\bigr)},
  \quad \varepsilon = 10^{-6},
  \qquad \tilde{c}_\tau \in [0, 1].
  \label{eq:norm_rate}
\end{equation}
%
The normalized change rate is mapped to per-step loss weights:
%
\begin{equation}
  w_\tau = 0.5 + \tilde{c}_\tau \in [0.5,\, 1.5].
  \label{eq:step_weight}
\end{equation}
%
The most rapidly changing step receives the highest weight $1.5$, while the smoothest step
receives the lowest weight $0.5$. The reweighted flow-matching loss applies the weight to
the per-step squared error:
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}}
  = \mathbb{E}_{t,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}
    \left[
      \frac{1}{T_a} \sum_{\tau=1}^{T_a}
      w_\tau \,
      \bigl\|\hat{\mathbf{v}}_\tau - \mathbf{u}_{t,\tau}\bigr\|_2^2
    \right].
  \label{eq:weighted_loss}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Boundary Prediction Head}

To let the model explicitly model the transition structure inside an action chunk, we
attach a lightweight \textbf{chunk boundary head} on top of the action features. Let
$\mathbf{f}_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ denote the per-step action feature produced after
the transformer's final layer norm (the same feature used to decode the velocity field).
The boundary head is a two-layer MLP:
%
\begin{equation}
  \hat{r}_\tau
  = \mathbf{W}_2\,\operatorname{SiLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{f}_\tau),
  \quad
  \mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{(d_h/2) \times d_h},\
  \mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (d_h/2)}.
  \label{eq:boundary_head}
\end{equation}
%
Its output layer weights and bias are zero-initialized so that boundary predictions start
neutral. The head regresses the stop-gradient normalized change rate $\tilde{c}_\tau$:
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{bnd}}
  = \frac{1}{T_a}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T_a}
    \bigl(\hat{r}_\tau - \operatorname{sg}(\tilde{c}_\tau)\bigr)^2,
  \label{eq:boundary_loss}
\end{equation}
%
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator, ensuring the boundary
supervision does not back-propagate into the change rate itself.

\subsection{Total Objective}

The overall training loss is the sum of the weighted flow-matching loss and the auxiliary
boundary loss:
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \mathcal{L}_{\text{vel}} + \lambda\, \mathcal{L}_{\text{bnd}},
  \qquad \lambda = 0.1,
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
%
where $\lambda$ is the boundary auxiliary loss weight (default $0.1$). When adaptive
chunking is disabled, all $w_\tau \equiv 1$ and the boundary head is removed, recovering
the standard uniform-MSE objective and guaranteeing backward compatibility with the
baseline.
